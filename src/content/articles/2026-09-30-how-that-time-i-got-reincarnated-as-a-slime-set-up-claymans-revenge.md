> 📝 Spoilers Ahead for That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime up through the Walpurgis arc.

*Clayman* is, on the surface, one of the least sympathetic villains in *That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime*. A scheming Demon Lord who manipulated nations, puppeteered monsters and engineered wars from the shadows, he met a brutal end at Rimuru's hands during the Walpurgis council. So why does he now have his own spin-off, *Clayman's Revenge*? The answer lies in how deliberately the main series built him up as a character with his own grudges, ambitions and, crucially, a reason to want payback.

## The Villain Who Worked From The Shadows

From his earliest appearances, *Clayman* is established as the schemer among the Demon Lords. Where *Milim Nava* smashes things and *Guy Crimson* rules through raw presence, *Clayman* prefers manipulation. He orchestrates the assault on the Kingdom of Falmuth, stirs conflict between monster factions and treats entire nations as pieces on a board.

That methodical approach matters, because it gives him interiority. The audience sees him planning, boasting, gloating and, most importantly, seething. **Every failure he suffers is witnessed from his own perspective**, which is more narrative attention than most arc villains ever receive.

---

## The Humiliations That Piled Up

*Clayman's* downfall is a long chain of embarrassments. His carefully laid plans against *Rimuru* collapse one after another. The monsters he believed he controlled slip free of his influence. His masterstroke at Walpurgis, accusing *Rimuru* before the assembled Demon Lords, is turned inside out and used against him.

The final blow lands in spectacular fashion. *Rimuru*, newly awakened after devouring ten thousand souls, confronts *Clayman* directly at the council, and the fight is not even close.

> 🤯 Milim, the very Demon Lord Clayman believed he was controlling, reveals she was never under his spell at all  -  his entire trump card was a fantasy.

That revelation is the heart of the setup. *Clayman* spends the arc believing he holds *Milim* in his grasp, only to learn she played him from the beginning. He dies not just defeated, but utterly deceived, humiliated before his peers and discarded.

---

## Why A Revenge Story Was Inevitable

Here is where the main series' structure does the heavy lifting. *That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime* has always been generous with villain backstories, and *Clayman* is given enough screen time, motivation and personality that a story told from his side was viable. He is petty, vain and cruel, but he is also genuinely convinced of his own genius, and that combination makes him compelling rather than disposable.

The spin-off premise works precisely because the main series established **a villain with an ego, a grudge and a worldview that never accepted his own defeat**. A character that self-absorbed does not simply accept being a footnote in *Rimuru's* rise. Retelling and expanding his campaign from his perspective reframes every scheme the audience already watched unfold.

## What It Means Going Forward

*Clayman's Revenge* is a chance to see the Walpurgis era through the eyes of the loser, and that is a genuinely smart angle. The main series told viewers what *Clayman* did; the spin-off gets to explore **why he did it, what he believed he was owed, and how a mid-tier Demon Lord convinced himself he could topple Rimuru Tempest**.

For fans of the franchise, it deepens the best-constructed arc of the series. The scheming, the manipulation, the collapse at Walpurgis, it all reads differently when the schemer gets his own voice. The main series spent years setting the table. Now *Clayman* finally gets to tell his side.
