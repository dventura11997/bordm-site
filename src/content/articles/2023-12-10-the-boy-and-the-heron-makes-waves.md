<p>Variety reports that <em>Hayao Miyazaki's</em> latest masterpiece achieved outstanding numbers just one day after its North American debut, amassing an impressive <strong>$5.4 million on opening day</strong>, with a projected <strong>$10 million for the entire opening weekend.</strong></p>
<p>In a surprising turn of events, <a href="/trending-topics/the-boy-and-the-heron">"The Boy And The Heron"</a> is poised to claim the top spot in the domestic box office, having earned $5.4 million on Friday alone. Deadline's report underscores the <strong>film's historic success for Studio Ghibli in North America</strong>, outshining the debut of <em>"The Wind Rises,"</em> which previously held the record with <strong>$5.2 million.</strong></p>

<p><em>"The Boy And The Heron"</em> continues to add to <em>Studio Ghibli's</em> illustrious portfolio, projected to secure its place as the highest-grossing film of the weekend, with an opening day of <strong>$5.4 million</strong>.</p>
<blockquote>🤯 It is expected to surpass the $10 million mark by the end of the weekend, outperforming contenders like "The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes" ($9.4 million) and "Godzilla: Minus One" ($7.9 million). This success reflects the global impact of anime, marking a significant milestone for the genre.</blockquote>
<p><em>Hayao Miyazaki's</em> penultimate film received an impressive CinemaScore of A-, contributing to its overall success. <strong>With a total revenue of $84 million, $56 million of which comes from Japan</strong>, <em>"The Boy and The Heron"</em> stands as a testament to <em>Studio Ghibli's</em> international acclaim. Notably, the film achieved this without significant pre-release marketing, underscoring the enduring global popularity of the Studio Ghibli brand, reminiscent of the success of classics like <strong>"Spirited Away" and "Howl's Moving Castle."</strong></p>

<p>Despite a lack of pre-release marketing, the film's debut was met with resounding success, screening in over <strong>2000 cinemas on its first day.</strong> Building on the recent success of anime films in North American theaters, exemplified by Ufotable's <a href="/trending-topics/demon-slayer">"Demon Slayer: Mugen Train,"</a> which earned <strong>$49 million</strong>, <em>"The Boy And The Heron"</em> demonstrates that its triumph is not solely attributable to <em>Hayao Miyazaki's</em> name but also to the synergy of nostalgic artistry, a stellar cast, and a compelling storyline, proving that <em>Studio Ghibli's</em> magic continues to captivate audiences worldwide.</p>
<p>"The Boy and The Heron" is currently enchanting audiences in theaters.</p>
