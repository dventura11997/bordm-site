> 📝 Spoilers Ahead for the Spirit Circle manga

*Fuuta Okeya* looks like an ordinary 14-year-old, apart from the bandage on his cheek and the ghosts he casually sees drifting past him every day. Then a transfer student named *Kouko Ishigami* notices the strange mark hidden beneath that bandage, and everything changes. **She declares that the two of them are connected across multiple past lives, and she wants him dead.** With an 18-episode anime adaptation airing in December, now is the perfect time to unpack how this fascinating reincarnation mechanic actually works.

## The Mark Beneath The Bandage

At the centre of the story sits the **spirit circle** itself: a mechanism through which *Fuuta* experiences his own past lives. The mark on his cheek is a physical remnant of his soul's long history, and it's the very thing *Kouko* recognises. She carries the same kind of connection, and she identifies *Fuuta* as the reincarnation of someone who wronged her across many lifetimes.

This isn't a simple grudge. It's hatred so deep that it has survived death itself, carried forward through reincarnation after reincarnation. *Kouko* isn't hunting a stranger; she's hunting a soul.

---

## Living Through Past Lives

Here's where the series gets clever. Rather than simply telling you about these past lives, *Spirit Circle* **shows them to you**, one at a time. Through the spirit circle, *Fuuta* is plunged into the memories of his previous incarnations, experiencing each life from the inside: their choices, their regrets, and their fates.

Each past life is essentially its own self-contained story, complete with its own setting, cast, and tragedy. And crucially, *Kouko* appears in these lives too, in different forms and roles. Sometimes she's an enemy. Sometimes she's something far more complicated. Watching the same two souls collide across different eras and circumstances is the engine that drives the entire narrative.

> 🤯 The past lives aren't just backstory; they're a chain of evidence, and Fuuta is living through every piece of it.

This structure does something remarkable for the central conflict. *Fuuta* can't dismiss *Kouko's* hatred as irrational, because he comes to understand exactly where it comes from. He experiences the deeds that fuelled it firsthand, sometimes from perspectives he'd rather forget.

---

## A Grudge Versus Understanding

*Satoshi Mizukami*, also known for *Lucifer and the Biscuit Hammer* and *Sengoku Youko*, has always been drawn to stories where enemies are forced to genuinely know each other. *Spirit Circle* is that idea taken to its absolute limit. **The question at the heart of the series isn't whether Kouko can kill Fuuta. It's whether understanding across lifetimes can dissolve a grudge that death itself couldn't end.**

Every past life *Fuuta* experiences reframes the present. Alliances shift, sympathies reverse, and the line between victim and villain blurs with each incarnation. It's a story that asks whether people are defined by their souls or by the choices they make in each individual life.

---

## What This Means For The Anime

An 18-episode run, directed by the late *Tatsuo Sato* as his final work, gives the adaptation real room to breathe across these distinct past-life arcs. The structure is demanding: each arc demands new settings and tones while keeping the central thread between *Fuuta* and *Kouko* taut.

If the anime honours the manga's structure, viewers are in for something genuinely rare: a revenge story where the protagonist is forced to earn his enemy's forgiveness by understanding it, one lifetime at a time. *Spirit Circle* is a cult favourite for good reason, and December should show everyone exactly why.
