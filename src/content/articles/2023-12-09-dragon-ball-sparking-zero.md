<p>Spearheaded by <em>Spike Chunsoft</em>, the studio celebrated for the original <em>Budokai Tenkaichi</em> series, <strong>Dragon Ball Sparking Zero</strong> is being crafted using <strong>Unreal Engine 5</strong>. The game is set to make its mark on <strong>PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, and PC</strong>. True to its predecessors, the upcoming release promises an expansive roster covering the manga and anime's extensive history, along with massive destructible arenas and eye-catching automatic combos. <em>Bandai Namco</em>, the publisher, tantalizingly hints at "new genre-defining features."</p>

<p>Diverging from the 2D realm of <em>Dragon Ball Z fighting games, the Budokai Tenkaichi</em> series unfolds within 3D landscapes. Here, players can soar through the air, lock onto opponents, and send them crashing into mountains and craters. The battles may get chaotic, but they authentically capture the essence, speed, and dynamism of the anime across a vast array of characters.</p>
<p>Since the last instalment in 2007, fans have patiently awaited a true successor, even in the face of impressive alternatives like <em>Dragon Ball FighterZ (See Figure Below)</em>. <strong>Dragon Ball Sparking Zero has significant expectations to meet</strong>, aiming to fill the sizeable shoes left vacant for years. While the game is anticipated to showcase stunning visuals on modern consoles, the challenge lies in preserving the chaotic allure of its predecessors while delivering a polished and precise experience that aligns with contemporary fighting game sensibilities.</p>
