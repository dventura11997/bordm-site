<blockquote>📝 Spoilers Ahead for Demon Slayer's Manga</blockquote>
<p>Muzan Kibutsuji, the formidable antagonist in <a href="/trending-topics/demon-slayer">Demon Slayer</a>, not only orchestrates the challenges that confront our heroes but also serves as the genesis of nearly every demon encountered. His unique ability to spawn demons raises an intriguing question: <strong>if Muzan created the demons in the series, who, in turn, created Muzan?</strong></p>
<p>While <em>Demon Slayer</em> unfolds in the early 20th century, <em>Muzan's</em> existence <strong>spans back a millennium</strong>, revealed through flashbacks. His modus operandi involves transforming humans with his blood, endowing them with formidable powers, provided they can endure the radical metamorphosis.</p>
<p>Those unable to withstand the changes find themselves reduced to a puddle of goo, ultimately turning to dust. <em>Muzan</em> wields a surprising level of control over those he transforms, effortlessly dispatching them for mere infractions like uttering his name.</p>
<hr>
<h2>The Unintentional Transformation of Demon Slayer's Muzan</h2>

<p>In <strong>Chapter 127</strong> of the <em>Demon Slayer</em> manga, it is unveiled that <em>Muzan's</em> journey into demonhood wasn't preordained; however, he was never a paragon of virtue. Like most demons, <em>Muzan</em> originated as a human. Yet, being the first of his kind, he lacked the convenience of another demon imparting blood for his transformation;</p>
<blockquote>🤯 Muzan's metamorphosis into a demon was a serendipitous accident.</blockquote>
<p>Around the early 900s, a millennium before the events in the story, <em>Muzan</em>, a human suffering from an unspecified ailment, opted for an experimental treatment to stave off death. The concoction, derived from the rare <strong>Blue Spider Lily</strong>, seemingly healed him. <strong>However, it barred him from exposure to sunlight and triggered other demonic manifestations.</strong> Enraged, <em>Muzan</em> slew the doctor responsible for the experimental medicine but remained incapable of tolerating sunlight.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Muzan's Pivotal Quest for the Blue Spider Lily</h2>
<p>Since that transformative incident, <em>Muzan</em> has pursued two primary objectives. First, <strong>he seeks to proliferate demons by sharing his blood,</strong> <a href="/articles/what-makes-nezuko-impervious-to-sunlight-in-demon-slayer">hoping that one might develop a tolerance to sunlight</a>, thereby revealing the secret to him. Second, he relentlessly pursues the <strong>Blue Spider Lily</strong>, aiming to recreate the original serum that transformed him, potentially restoring his ability to withstand sunlight. <em>Muzan's</em> evident interest in chemistry and science throughout the manga stems from his millennium-long quest for knowledge that might help him conquer this vulnerability.</p>

<p>Despite his pursuits, <em>Muzan</em> is adamant about not reverting to a human existence in <em>Demon Slayer</em>. Having once grappled with the looming specter of mortality, he rejects the prospect of returning to it. Instead, he strives for immortality by eliminating his sole significant weakness.</p>
<p><em>Muzan's</em> intricate motivations and backstory in <em>Demon Slayer</em> add depth to his character, challenging expectations for a character portrayed as cold and cruel. <strong>The poignant irony lies in the fact that the elusive Blue Spider Lily blooms during the day</strong>, albeit rarely. Thus, regardless of how many millennia <em>Muzan</em> spent searching, he was fated never to discover it in the world of <em>Demon Slayer.</em></p>
