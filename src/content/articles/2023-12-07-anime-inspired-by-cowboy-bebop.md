<h2>Exploring the Timeless Appeal of '90s Anime</h2>
<p>The '90s marked a period of transformative change, not only within the anime realm but also in the broader global context. Consequently, many Japanese series from this era delve into themes that feel remarkably relevant today, pushing the boundaries of animation conventions. This enduring quality has helped these anime maintain their appeal well beyond two decades.</p>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Cowboy Bebop" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/65716f612ea72df329e0de85_l-intro-1661956846.webp">
  <figcaption>Cowboy Bebop</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>So if you've always been a fan of Anime from the 90's such as Cowboy Bebop and more, here is a list of other popular 90's anime's you can enjoy that maintain that Cowboy Bebop style of animation.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Sailor Moon (1992)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Sailor Moon" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/65716f6e45f2a17b4c302c19_Screen-Shot-2023-06-05-at-10.10.14-am.webp">
  <figcaption>Sailor Moon</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>Despite the shonen genre often taking the spotlight as anime's primary powerhouse, the influence of shojo series like <em>Sailor Moon</em> cannot be overlooked. <strong>Premiering in 1992</strong>, this beloved series swiftly ascended to become one of the world's most popular animated shows within a decade.</p>
<p><em>Sailor Moon</em> chronicles the journey of <em>Usagi Tsukino</em>, a young schoolgirl bestowed <strong>with the ability to transform into the superhero Sailor Moon</strong>. Alongside her fellow Sailor Soldiers, they embark on a quest to vanquish the forces of the malevolent Dark Kingdom. The timeless allure of Sailor Moon persists, transcending its initial era.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Dragon Ball Z (1989)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Dragon Ball Z" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/65716f8882dfecc9c1e939f6_t9xtMju7CHFDFSw3uBYz26.jpg">
  <figcaption>Dragon Ball Z</figcaption>
</figure>
<p><a href="/trending-topics/dragon-ball">Dragon Ball Z</a> stands as a titan that significantly shaped not only the shonen genre but an entire decade of Japanese media. Although technically <strong>debuting in 1989, the majority of its 291 episodes, along with most films, graced the following decade</strong>. Despite dated animation, the series resonates with the essence of modern shonen anime, embodying qualities that would define the genre for years to come.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Detective Conan (1996)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Detective Conan" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/65716fa133cc4d93c22ccdf0_detective-conan-kurogane-no-submarine-visual.webp">
  <figcaption>Detective Conan</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>In an era where police anime typically enjoyed only brief runs, <em>Detective Conan</em> defied conventions. <strong>Launched in 1996</strong>, the series continues to be syndicated decades later, showcasing its enduring popularity. The narrative follows <em>Shinichi Kudo</em>, a detective transformed into an elementary school child named <em>Conan Edogawa</em>, as he humorously aids in apprehending villains. <em>Detective Conan's</em> timeless appeal has generated laughs and suspense <strong>over a staggering 1,000 episodes.</strong></p>
<hr>
<h2>Pokémon (1997)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Pokémon" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/65716fae3f125243c666dd7c_maxresdefault.webp">
  <figcaption>Pokémon</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>For a generation of anime enthusiasts, especially those born in the '90s, the <a href="/trending-topics/pokemon">Pokémon</a> franchise served as a gateway into the medium. Boasting unparalleled popularity that <strong>spans over 25 years</strong>, the series remains a constant source of new episodes. While <em>Ash Ketchum</em> may have recently retired, the adventures with <em>Pikachu</em> continue to be an indelible part of childhoods worldwide, with over <strong>1,000 episodes chronicling their journey.</strong></p>
<hr>
<h2>Hunter X Hunter (1999)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Hunter X Hunter" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/65716fda52870468b36e156c_MV5BMGU1OWFjNGMtZTVkNS00NDcxLWI0YmEtMDgxYjBmMDdlNTRkXkEyXkFqcGdeQXVyNjAwNDUxODI%40._V1_.webp">
  <figcaption>Hunter X Hunter</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>While the <a href="/trending-topics/hunterxhunter">Hunter x Hunter</a> franchise is widely known, the <strong>1999</strong> original holds a distinct charm, setting a darker tone compared to <strong>its 2011 reboot</strong>. With 62 episodes released before a decade-long hiatus, the '99 version stands as a unique experience. As the Hunter x Hunter manga resumes periodic releases, now is an opportune time to explore the franchise's initial venture into the anime landscape.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Slam Dunk (1993)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Slam Dunk" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/65716fffb9e32dd5c95c9d8a_s.webp">
  <figcaption>Slam Dunk</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>Sports anime often face skepticism, but <em>Slam Dunk</em> breaks the mold. This iconic basketball series, reigning over the mid-'90s anime scene, remains relevant. The manga's continued success and a feature-length film in 2022 attest to its enduring popularity. <strong>As one of the first sports anime to achieve mainstream success</strong>, Slam Dunk defies stereotypes within the genre.</p>
<hr>
<h2>One Piece (1999)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="One Piece" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/657170320888a8c72a0ab5f7_one-piece-original-ova.webp">
  <figcaption>One Piece</figcaption>
</figure>
<p><em>Eiichiro Oda's</em> masterpiece, <a href="/trending-topics/one-piece">One Piece</a>, has cultivated a legacy that expands with each passing year. <strong>Debuting in 1999</strong>, the pirate-themed shonen continues to thrive. Currently embarking on its <strong>final saga after two decades</strong>, the series stands unparalleled in the realm of long-running success. One Piece adeptly adapts its art style and animation, evident in the latest episodes of the Wano Country Arc, showcasing its evolution.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Neon Genesis Evangelion (1995)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Neon Genesis Evangelion" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/6571704e88651712f0793cb6_21ba6e8632112d17080a598a48f3001a.webp">
  <figcaption>Neon Genesis Evangelion</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>In the mid-'90s, anime's future in Western markets faced uncertainties. <em>Neon Genesis Evangelion</em> shattered preconceptions with its avant-garde presentation and profound themes, elevating anime's maturity among Western audiences. Over 25 years later, this legendary title maintains its impressive stature, a testament to its enduring impact.</p>
<hr>
<h2>Yu Yu Hakusho: Ghost Files (1992)</h2>
<figure class="article-image">
  <img loading="lazy" alt="Yu Yu Hakusho: Ghost Files" src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/650e88099aad1a83c37df57d/6571705a9ddc87433e13cfc7_3d68566aa58fa381b10f1b9d2c9ebd46.webp">
  <figcaption>Yu Yu Hakusho: Ghost Files</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>Long before the era of Shonen Jump's <strong>"Big Three,"</strong> <em>Yoshihiro Togashi's Yu Yu Hakusho</em> laid the groundwork for the shonen genre's dominance. Following the story of <em>Yuusuke Urameshi</em>, a delinquent turned <strong>"Spirit Detective,"</strong> the series influences modern anime profoundly. With its compelling episodes, <em>Yu Yu Hakusho</em> remains a delightful watch for dedicated shonen enthusiasts.</p>
